# Guided Templates and Diagnostics

Read this reference when the user asks for a fill-in template, is new to research writing, or wants a research-depth diagnosis.

## Results Subsection Template

1. **Scientific question:** We wanted to determine ___
2. **Why it matters:** This matters because ___
3. **Experiment or analysis:** To answer it, we ___
4. **Data:** The direct observations were ___
5. **Finding:** These data show ___
6. **1-hop implication:** This result suggests ___
7. **New question:** The finding raises ___
8. **Answerability:** The current study can / cannot answer it because ___
9. **Next placement:** New Results / Discussion / Limitation / Future Study

## Discussion Template

### Opening: integrated 2-hop interpretation

- Taken together, the findings show ___
- Beyond any single result, this means for the present study ___

Keep this paragraph close to the evidence in the current study.

### Middle: cautious abstraction

- More broadly, these findings suggest the possible principle that ___
- This principle may apply beyond the current system to ___, provided that ___

### New question space

- Why might the principle hold?
- How could the mechanism operate?
- What factor determines its strength?
- When and where should it hold or fail?
- Whether does it generalize after relevant controls?
- To what extent is the effect robust?

### Limitation and future study

- The current study cannot answer ___ because ___
- A future study could answer it by ___

## Research Depth Diagnostic

- **Level 0 — Observation:** reports only that A differs from B.
- **Level 1 — Implication:** states what the difference directly means.
- **Level 2 — Interpretation:** proposes why or how the effect occurs.
- **Level 3 — General principle:** offers a transferable abstraction.
- **Level 4 — Boundary or mechanism questions:** asks when it holds, what controls magnitude, why it fails, and where counterexamples may occur.
- **Level 5 — Research program:** defines experiments that distinguish competing hypotheses and map the principle's scope.

Diagnose the current level separately for each major claim. Do not reward abstraction unsupported by evidence. Recommend the smallest next step that raises the study's explanatory depth.

## Evidence-Calibrated Language

Use wording that matches support:

- Directly measured: “showed,” “was associated with,” “increased under these conditions.”
- Mechanism suggested but not isolated: “is consistent with,” “suggests,” “may reflect.”
- Generalization not tested: “raises the possibility,” “remains to be tested.”
- Alternative explanations remain: name them explicitly and avoid causal phrasing.

The purpose of the templates is to expose reasoning gaps. Do not preserve the fill-in format when polished prose or a decision table would better serve the user.
